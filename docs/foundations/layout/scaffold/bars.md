# Scaffold

A fundamental UI design structure that provides a standard platform for assembling key components

## Bars

## Bars

Bars frame the screen to help people navigate through a product. They typically contain an app bar or bottom navigation bar.

Bars can span a single pane or across the full width of a window.

![Navigation bar on mobile and app bar on desktop.](../../../../assets/images/1ee7d60cfd68-mp3nck82-01.png)

_A navigation bar occupies the bottom bar region on mobileAn app bar occupies the top bar region on the web_

App bars are placed at the top of the screen to help people navigate, providing a title and 1–2 essential actions like search or back.

![A photo app with an app bar below the safety region and above a grid of photos.](../../../../assets/images/3dbf9ba5fd13-mp3nejq3-02.png)

_The app bar sits at the top of the screen, outside of the safety region_

Navigation bars let people switch between 3–5 primary UI views at compact or medium breakpoints.

![A photo app with a navigation bar below a grid of photos.](../../../../assets/images/8378e5a066f6-mp3ng1v5-03.png)

_The navigation bar sits at the bottom of the screen, above the safety region_

### Safety region

Bars are placed adjacent to the safety regions, which contain [system UI](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/system/system-bars) elements.

The safety region shouldn’t contain primary content.

![A photo app with a safety region above an app bar and grid of photos.](../../../../assets/images/a407091ba6f6-mp3nk11i-04.png)

_The safety region—at the top and bottom edges of the screen on compact devices—protect system UI elements_
